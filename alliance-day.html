Elections · Session 05 · Alliance Day

Who could we
actually work with?

Find common ground.
Identify differences.
Decide who you could work with.

Work with your party. Use concrete policies as evidence. You are exploring possible cooperation, not forming an official coalition.

Five questions for your meetings
  • What policy do we have in common?
  • Where do our parties disagree?
  • Could we support one of your policies?
  • Could you support one of ours?
  • What could we realistically work together on?
Teacher quick start · Organise the meetings
  1. Party HQ · 5 minutes: review policies, earlier contacts and possible conflicts. Each party chooses three other parties to meet.
  2. Alliance Meetings · 20–25 minutes: organise three rounds of about 6–7 minutes. Confirm the meetings and representatives so groups do not all wait for the same party. The choices on this page are preferences, not an automatic timetable. With seven parties, split representatives when needed.
  3. After each meeting, record one agreement, one disagreement and YES / MAYBE / NO for cooperation. Discuss a possible shared proposal aloud; no formal coalition is needed.
  4. Return to HQ · 10 minutes: compare evidence and classify the other parties. Leave a party unclassified if there is not enough evidence yet.
  5. Final decision · 10 minutes: choose a best possible ally, a shared policy, a possible joint project and a main rival with one major disagreement. Each party closes with a 30–45 second statement.

Use “Project meeting questions” to display just the five prompts. Students can use paper or one device per group. Notes save only in that browser, with no live classroom synchronisation.

The outcomes will support Coalition Challenge and Rival Debate Day. This session does not make alliances official.
