What makes a developed answer?

Question: Why should young people receive cheaper public transport?

Weak: Because it is expensive.

What we want:

Young people usually have less money and many of them depend on public transport to study or work. For example, a student who travels every day can spend a significant part of their money on transport. Cheaper fares would make education and work more accessible.

Do not memorise this example. Use the structure: ANSWER + WHY + EXAMPLE.

Teacher quick start · Run this session
  1. Briefing · 5 minutes: explain the answer structure and the no-full-scripts rule.
  2. Preparation · 10 minutes: each group selects its party, reads all three questions and prepares ideas together. Notes are optional.
  3. Conference · 3–4 minutes per party: select the party on the classroom screen and start. Read or project question 1, listen to the developed answer, then move to questions 2 and 3.
  4. Use a different speaker for each question whenever possible. A fourth student can add information, give another example or complete an answer.
  5. Finish the conference, then choose the next party.

The timer is optional and never advances the activity automatically. It covers the whole conference, not each question. Preparation notes stay on the device where they were written; groups can also use paper. Close the private notebook before projecting.
