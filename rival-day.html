Rival Debate Day.

Same problem. Different solution.
Defend yours.

You must · Every student
  1. State your position.
  2. Give a reason or example.
  3. React to the other party.
  4. Ask or answer a question.
  5. Challenge one of their ideas.
  6. Speak at least 3 times.

Across your turns, include one argument, one reaction/rebuttal and one question or answer. Do not repeat the same idea. Do not read a full script. Listen to the other team.

A debate is not two presentations. LISTEN and RESPOND.
Language help · Challenge and rebut

I disagree because…
The problem with that argument is…
You said that…, but…

Direct challenge questions

  • Who would be negatively affected by your policy?
  • Why should your group receive priority?
  • What would happen if your proposal failed?
  • Is your idea fair for people outside your target group?
  • How would this work in practice?
Teacher quick start & assessment
  1. Assign rivals · 5 minutes: use known conflicts from earlier sessions. Choose a suggested pairing or “Custom debate”. The suggestions are options, not a timetable: arrange enough debates for every party to participate.
  2. Prepare · 10 minutes: each party records keywords for its position, two arguments, an example, a weakness in the rival’s position and one question.
  3. Debate · about 6–8 minutes for two parties: use the next-turn button. Opening positions take 30–40 seconds per party. Each argument is followed by a rival’s response; then questions, answers, rebuttals and the compromise question.
  4. Rotate speakers within each phase. Each student must speak at least three times, including an argument, a reaction/rebuttal and a question or answer. Several students may share a phase. Allow extra exchanges or extend the time for larger groups and three-party debates.
  5. Other groups record only the strongest argument, best rebuttal and possible compromise in Observers.

For three parties, every argument gets two responses. Direct questions follow A → B → C → A. No agreement is required at the end.
