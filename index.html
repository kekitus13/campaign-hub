Speaking project · First session

Campaign Hub.

Know your party. Defend your ideas. Find common ground.
Work in English, use keywords for notes and respect your party’s register.

New GenerationExperience MattersWorking LifeDigital TomorrowLearning TogetherOur CommunityThe Everyday Voice
01Meet your party

Look at your party’s infographic and briefly discuss it together.

  • Who do you represent?
  • What register should you use?
  • What do your six policies mean?
Make sure everyone understands the party before moving on.
02Choose your main idea

Agree on the central idea that represents your party. Then share it with the other parties.

Our party mainly believes that…
03Know your six policies

Discuss these four questions for each of your six policies. Write keywords only.

  • WHAT? What are we proposing?
  • WHY? Why do we think it is necessary?
  • WHO? Who would benefit?
  • CHALLENGE? What might another party question or disagree with?
Everyone should be able to explain the policies in their own words.
04Create two new policies

Suggest different ideas, discuss them and reach a consensus on two new policies.

  • Do they fit your party’s identity?
  • Do they address the needs of the people you represent?
  • Can you explain and defend them?
Listen to everyone’s suggestions before deciding together.
05Political speed meetings

Split up and meet representatives from other parties. Each round lasts five minutes. Follow your teacher’s instructions for changing partners.

  • Explain your proposals and ask about theirs.
  • Find something you agree on.
  • Explore a disagreement or a question.
  • Discuss whether cooperation could work.
Keep brief notes: Party · Agreement · Problem · Possible cooperation
06Back to headquarters

Return to your party. Everyone reports what they discovered in their meetings.

  • Which party could be your best ally?
  • Why would that alliance make sense?
  • Which policies could you work on together?
Use what you learned in the meetings to reach a group decision.
07Your campaign statement

Finish with a 30-second campaign statement to the class.

  • Who are you?
  • What do you stand for?
  • What should people remember about your party?
Your statement should sound like YOUR party. Use the appropriate register.
